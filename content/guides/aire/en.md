:::tip[In short]
This guide is for **Italian citizens** living in Sweden. It is written in English for partners, Swedish relatives and English-speaking Italians. If an Italian citizen moves abroad **for more than 12 months**, they must register in **AIRE** (the Italian register of citizens living abroad) **within 90 days**. It is done **online** on the **FAST IT** portal, with a certificate from **Skatteverket** no more than 3 months old. Without AIRE there is no passport from the Embassy, no transcription of births and marriages, and a child born in Sweden cannot have Italian citizenship recorded.
:::

**See also:** voting in [the right to vote](/en/guide/rostratt) and, if you move back to Italy one day, [leaving Sweden](/en/guide/flytta-fran-sverige).

## What AIRE is and who must register

AIRE is the Italian register of citizens who live outside Italy. You must register if you **move your residence abroad for more than 12 months**. It also applies to people who are already abroad because they were born there or became Italian later.

The deadline is **90 days** from the move. Registering in AIRE **removes you from the Italian municipality's population register** (APR).

AIRE is not the same as Swedish registration. You do [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing) with Skatteverket and AIRE with Italy. They are two separate registrations, and the first gives you the document you need for the second.

## How to register, step by step

1. **Register in Sweden.** You need [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing) first.
2. **Order the right certificate from Skatteverket.** It is called **"Utdrag med familjära relationer samt datum på flytt till/från Sverige"**. It must be **in your name** and issued **no more than 3 months ago**.
3. **Log in to the FAST IT portal** (serviziconsolari.esteri.it) and fill in the request. The portal generates the form; if you log in with **SPID** no signature is needed.
4. **Attach the documents:** Italian ID card or passport (recently expired is fine) and the Skatteverket certificate.

For families, you need **one certificate per adult Italian citizen**.

Email to the Embassy is only a fallback if the portal does not work.

:::who[What you need]
- Italian ID card or passport
- Skatteverket certificate "Utdrag med familjära relationer samt datum på flytt till/från Sverige", in your name, **max 3 months old**
- Access to FAST IT (with SPID, no signature)
- One certificate per adult Italian citizen in the family
:::

## If you do not register

The consulate can pass on your registration **ex officio** when it appears that you live abroad permanently. Do not count on that: register yourself within 90 days.

The Italian **law of 30 December 2023, no. 213** introduced **administrative sanctions** for people who do not register or do not update their data. They are applied by the Italian **comune**. We state no amounts: ask your comune.

Once registered, you must tell the AIRE comune about **every change**: civil status, address, household composition.

## What changes when you register

- **Healthcare.** You lose your enrolment in the Italian national health service, meaning your **GP** in Italy. When you are in Italy, **urgent care** remains. In Sweden you are covered **as a resident**: see the guide on [healthcare](/en/guide/vard).
- **Voting.** People registered in AIRE vote **by post from abroad** in general elections and referendums. You can choose to vote in Italy if you say so within the deadlines: **check the Embassy website before each election**.
- **Tax.** Since 2024, AIRE registration **alone no longer decides** your tax residence (reform of art. 2 TUIR, d.lgs. 209/2023): what counts is where you actually live. **Talk to an accountant** (commercialista).
- **A home in Italy.** If you own one, ask the **comune** how your position changes. We give no rules here.

## Passport and electronic ID card (CIE)

**Passport.** For people who live in Sweden and are **registered in AIRE**. You need the form, the old passport, **two photos** and an updated family certificate from Skatteverket. Pay by card or in cash in kronor; **Swish is not available**. The **fee changes every quarter**: see the table on the Embassy website. We give no figure.

If you live in **Skåne**, you can start the process at the **honorary consulate in Malmö**.

**CIE.** For people who live in the Embassy's district and are registered in AIRE. You book through Prenot@mi, the fee is updated every quarter and the card is sent **by post**. **According to the Embassy**, from 1 June 2026 people registered in AIRE can apply for the CIE in any Italian comune. Check the Embassy website before planning a trip.

## Civil status: births, marriages, divorces

Births, marriages, divorces and deaths that happen **in Sweden** must be **transcribed in Italy** through the Embassy. **AIRE registration must come first.** Without transcription you cannot get updated Italian certificates and documents, and changes of civil status must be declared (DPR 396/2000).

Requests go **by post** to: *Italienska Ambassaden, "Stato Civile", Djurgårdsvägen 174, 115 21 Stockholm*.

If your Swedish documents need translation or legalisation, see the Embassy's [page on translation and legalisation](https://ambstoccolma.esteri.it/servizi-consolari-e-visti/servizi-per-il-cittadino-italiano/traduzione-e-legalizzazione-dei-documenti/). We give no general rules.

## Children born in Sweden

This is where people go wrong most often. The Embassy says it plainly: for births abroad, **check the citizenship requirements first**.

**Swedish at birth.** Under Swedish law (2001:82, § 2) the child is Swedish if **one parent is a Swedish citizen**, wherever the child is born. See the guide on [Swedish citizenship](/en/guide/medborgarskap).

**Italian by descent.** This is not automatic for everyone.

:::warn[The 2025 reform tightened the rules]
**Decree-law 36/2025**, converted into **law 74/2025** (in force from 24 May 2025), limits automatic recognition for people **born abroad who also hold another citizenship**. It remains automatic if:

- a **parent was born in Italy**, or
- a **grandparent was born in Italy**, or
- a **citizen parent lived in Italy for at least 2 years in a row** before the birth.

The Constitutional Court (ruling 63/2026) upheld the limit; in July 2026 a question was referred to the EU Court of Justice. So the picture may still move. More in the [sister site's guide to Italian citizenship](https://italia-pratica.it/en/guide/cittadinanza).
:::

**The typical case in Stockholm.** A child is born in Sweden to one Italian and one Swedish parent: the child is **Swedish from birth** and is also entitled to Italian citizenship **if the conditions above are met** (for example, the Italian parent was born in Italy). That is dual citizenship, and both Sweden and Italy allow it.

The **4 practical steps**:

1. The **Italian parent is registered in AIRE**.
2. You request the **transcription of the birth certificate** through the Embassy (civil status office), with the Skatteverket certificate.
3. The **child is registered in AIRE** with the parent.
4. Then **Italian passport or CIE**.

:::danger[If the Italian parent was born outside Italy]
If they were born abroad and have not lived 2 years in Italy, the rules are stricter. **Check with the Embassy or a lawyer before you start collecting documents.** For exact deadlines and documents for the transcription, ask the Embassy.
:::

## Moving back to Italy

You register with the Italian comune's population register and AIRE is updated. Contact the comune, and the Embassy if needed, before you leave.

## Common mistakes

:::danger[The mistakes that cost you]
- **Not registering in AIRE** despite more than 12 months in Sweden: administrative sanctions (law 213/2023), and without registration no passport from the Embassy and no transcriptions.
- **Having a child's birth transcribed without first checking the citizenship requirements**, especially if the Italian parent was born abroad.
:::

- **The wrong or an old Skatteverket certificate.** It is called "Utdrag med familjära relationer samt datum på flytt till/från Sverige" and must be no more than 3 months old.
- **Forgetting one certificate per adult Italian citizen** when registering a family.
- **Turning up at the Embassy without an appointment.** It receives people only through Prenot@mi.
- **Assuming AIRE alone settles your tax.** Not since 2024: talk to a commercialista.
- **Not reporting changes** (address, civil status, family) to the AIRE comune.
- **Looking for figures in this guide.** Fees and sanctions change: current amounts are on the Embassy website.
