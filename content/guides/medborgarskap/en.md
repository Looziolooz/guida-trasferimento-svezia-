:::tip[In short]
You are Swedish **at birth** if at least one parent is Swedish, wherever you are born. Everyone else goes through **naturalisation**: since 6 June 2026 that takes **8 years** of residence (shorter for some groups), a permanent permit, good conduct, **enough income** and knowledge of Swedish and Swedish society. **EU/EEA citizens have an important shortcut** (see below). You apply to Migrationsverket, the Swedish Migration Agency.
:::

**Voting:** voting in Riksdag elections requires citizenship: see [the right to vote](/en/guide/rostratt).

## How citizenship is acquired

There are two routes. **At birth**, without doing anything. **By naturalisation**, with an application to Migrationsverket once you meet the requirements. The rules are in the Swedish Citizenship Act (2001:82).

## At birth

A child is Swedish at birth if **a parent is a Swedish citizen** (or was one when they died). **Where the child is born does not matter**: the child of a Swede born in Rome, Nairobi or Lima is as Swedish as one born in Stockholm.

## By naturalisation from 6 June 2026

:::warn[New rules from 6 June 2026, no transitional rules]
The rules changed on **6 June 2026** and there are **no transitional rules**: applications already submitted are also decided under the new rules. Older guides no longer apply.
:::

Adults (from age 18) must meet all of these conditions.

- **Proven identity.**
- **A permanent residence permit.** Since 12 July 2026 permanent permits are generally no longer granted ([more in the permits guide](/en/guide/uppehallstillstand)): check Migrationsverket's 12 July notice for your situation. For EU/EEA citizens, see the box below.
- **Residence (*hemvist*) in Sweden for 8 years**, unless a shorter period applies (table below).
- **A conduct that is "skötsam och hederlig"**, meaning an orderly, honest life.
- **Self-support.**
- **Knowledge** of Swedish and of Swedish society.

:::note[EU/EEA citizens: the July change does not affect you]
For citizens of **EEA countries** and their family members, **the right of residence (*uppehållsrätt*) counts as a permanent permit** for citizenship purposes (§ 20 of the Act). So the end of permanent permits in July 2026 **does not concern you**: you do not need to apply for any permit. For Nordic citizens no permanent permit is needed at all.
:::

### Self-support

You need **income from work or a business of at least three *inkomstbasbelopp* a year**, which Migrationsverket puts at **about SEK 20,000 a month before tax**. The income must not be only temporary. You may also have received **no more than 6 months in total of social assistance** (*försörjningsstöd*) in the **3 years** before you apply. There are exceptions in some cases (pension, disability, studies): check Migrationsverket.

### Knowledge

- **Society:** shown through the **citizenship test** (*medborgarskapsprov i samhällskunskap*).
- **Swedish:** the **language test is not yet active**. For now you show your Swedish in other ways, for example school grades or SFI: check Migrationsverket for what is accepted (and see the guide to [SFI and work](/en/guide/sfi-och-arbete)).
- **Exemptions:** none required from age **67**. With sufficient grades in Danish or Norwegian you are exempt from the language part. There are also exceptions for disability or personal reasons.

## Shorter periods

The 8 years do not apply to everyone. The Act sets shorter periods for some:

| Who | Residence required |
|---|---|
| Citizens of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway; former Swedish citizens | **2 years** |
| Stateless people | **5 years** |
| Refugees | **7 years** |
| Married to or cohabiting with a Swedish citizen | **7 years**, **only if** the relationship has lasted at least 5 years **and** the partner has been Swedish for at least 5 years |
| Under 21 when applying | **7 years** |

What changed:

| | Before | Now (from 6 June 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic residence period | 5 years | **8 years** |
| Spouses of Swedish citizens | 3 years | **7 years**, with the two 5-year conditions |

If you do not meet both 5-year conditions, the ordinary 8 years apply.

## Children

For a minor, the application is made by **whoever has custody**. Since 2026 **each child has their own application**: the child is no longer added to a parent's. From age **12** the **child's consent** is required, and from **16** knowledge is required too. Residence periods are shorter for minors: check Migrationsverket for the exact ones.

## Losing citizenship at 22

If you were **born abroad**, **have never lived in Sweden** and have had no ties to the country, you **lose citizenship at age 22**, unless that would make you stateless. To **keep** it you can apply **between 18 and 22**. This mostly concerns children of Swedes who grow up outside Sweden: note the date.

## Dual citizenship

**Sweden allows dual citizenship.** Whether you can keep your other citizenship after naturalising is decided by **the other country**: check its rules. Italy, for example, allows it.

## Children with an Italian parent

*This section is for people with an Italian partner. If the other parent is from another country, the same logic applies: check that country's rules.*

A child born in Sweden to a Swedish parent and an Italian parent is **Swedish at birth** through the Swedish parent. The child may **also** be entitled to Italian citizenship, **but only within the conditions of the 2025 reform** (decree-law 36/2025, law 74/2025, in force since 24 May 2025). For people born abroad who hold another citizenship, recognition remains automatic if:

- **a parent was born in Italy**, or
- **a grandparent was born in Italy**, or
- **a citizen parent lived in Italy for at least 2 consecutive years** before the birth.

Italy's Constitutional Court (ruling 63/2026) has upheld the limit. If the Italian parent was born outside Italy and did not live there for 2 years, the rules are stricter: **check with the Embassy or a lawyer before gathering documents**.

To make the Italian citizenship show up in Italy, there is a set route:

1. the Italian parent must be **registered with AIRE** (see the [AIRE guide](/en/guide/aire));
2. you ask for the **transcription of the birth certificate** through the **Italian Embassy** (civil status office), with the Skatteverket certificate;
3. the child is registered with AIRE together with the parent;
4. then you can apply for an Italian passport or electronic ID card.

We have not been able to verify the exact deadlines and documents for the transcription: ask the Embassy. For Italian citizenship in detail, see the [sister guide on Italia Pratica](https://italia-pratica.it/en/guide/cittadinanza). The result is **dual citizenship**, which both countries allow.

## A proposal under discussion

A proposal is under discussion to **revoke citizenship** from people with dual citizenship who commit very serious crimes. It is only a proposal: it is not law today.

## Common mistakes

- **Using the rules from before 6 June 2026.** The 8 years also apply to applications already submitted.
- **Applying for a permanent permit as an EU/EEA citizen.** Not needed: your *uppehållsrätt* counts as one for citizenship.
- **Counting on 7 years as a spouse without checking the 5 + 5.** If the relationship or the partner's citizenship is under 5 years old, the period is 8 years.
- **Waiting for the language test.** It is not yet active: for now there are other ways.
- **Forgetting age 22.** A child of Swedes growing up abroad needs to apply to keep citizenship between 18 and 22.
- **Thinking Swedish citizenship cancels the other one.** The other country decides, not Sweden.
- **Assuming the child automatically gets Italian citizenship.** It depends on the 2025 conditions: check first.
