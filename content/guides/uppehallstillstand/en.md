:::tip[In short]
It depends on your passport. **EU/EEA/Swiss citizens:** you have a right of residence (*uppehållsrätt*) and **do not apply for anything** at Migrationsverket, the Swedish Migration Agency. **Non-EU family members:** you apply for a residence card (*uppehållskort*). **Everyone else:** you need a permit (*uppehållstillstånd*), for example a work permit, and for an employee permit the salary must be **at least SEK 34,470 a month**.
:::

## First, two different things

The **right to stay** says whether you may be in Sweden. **[Population registration](/en/guide/folkbokforing)** (*folkbokföring*), run by Skatteverket, the Swedish Tax Agency, says that you live here and gives you a personnummer. They are two procedures at two different agencies. This guide covers the first.

| | EU/EEA/Swiss citizen | Non-EU family member | Non-EU citizen |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Right of residence (*uppehållsrätt*) | Residence card (*uppehållskort*) | Permit (*uppehållstillstånd*) |
| **Application to Migrationsverket** | No | Yes, if you stay over 3 months | Yes |
| **After 5 years** | Permanent right | Check with Migrationsverket | Depends on the type of permit |

## EU, EEA and Swiss citizens

**Up to 3 months** you may stay with a valid passport or ID card and no other requirements.

**Beyond 3 months** you have a right of residence if you **work**, run your **own business**, **study**, or have **sufficient means** to support yourself. There is no form to fill in: as Migrationsverket puts it, if you have uppehållsrätt you do not need to contact them.

If you stay **at least a year**, you register with Skatteverket, and there you will have to *prove* your right of residence (an employment contract, savings and so on). That is covered in the guide to [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).

After **5 years** of uppehållsrätt you have a **permanent right of residence**. You can ask Migrationsverket for a certificate, free of charge.

:::note[Nordic citizens]
Citizens of Denmark, Finland, Norway and Iceland have their own, simpler rules. We do not summarise them here: see the Nordic citizens section on Migrationsverket's website.
:::

## Non-EU family members of an EU citizen

Are you the spouse, partner or family member of an EU/EEA citizen who has a right of residence in Sweden, but hold a third-country passport? If you stay **more than 3 months**, apply to Migrationsverket for a **residence card (uppehållskort)**, online or with form 144011. You can do this **from inside Sweden**. The card is valid for **up to 5 years**.

The official page is not clear about the fee, so check the current amount with Migrationsverket before you pay.

For family members of non-EU citizens (family reunification, *anknytning*), the rules were **tightened in 2026**. We cannot give you the details: check the rules in force on Migrationsverket before you plan the trip.

## Non-EU citizens

There are three main routes to a permit: **work** (as an employee or running your own business), **looking for work**, and **family ties** (*anknytning*).

### The employee work permit

You need a job offer and a high enough salary. The pay must be at least **90 % of the median wage**. Since 16 June 2026 the median is SEK 38,300, so the floor is **at least SEK 34,470 a month**. The median in force *on the day you apply* is the one that counts. The salary must also follow the collective agreement or industry practice. For some occupations the threshold is lower: see the list on Migrationsverket.

:::cost[Cost and timing]
**SEK 2,200** for the employee work permit application, **non-refundable** even if the answer is no.
**Complete application:** 75 % are decided within 4 months. **Incomplete application:** within 10 months.
The fee for a permit based on family ties has not been verified: see Migrationsverket's website.
:::

### The permit card

The permit comes as a card, the **uppehållstillståndskort**. Photo and fingerprints are given at the embassy if you need a visa or an in-person check. Many people do the passport check through an app instead.

For [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing) you need exactly this valid card: a permit from another EU country is not enough.

## 2026 changes

:::warn[Permanent permits: since 12 July 2026 no longer granted as a rule]
Since **12 July 2026** the permanent residence permit is **no longer granted as a general rule**. If you already hold one, nothing changes for you. The **long-term resident** status (*varaktigt bosatt*, EU long-term residence) still exists: you need **5 years** of residence, absences of no more than **6 months in a row or 10 months in total**, and means to support yourself in the long term.
:::

:::warn[Citizenship: since 6 June 2026 it takes 8 years]
Since **6 June 2026** citizenship requires **8 years** of continuous residence (it used to be 5), an income requirement, and knowledge of the Swedish language and society. The tests are being introduced from 2026–2027: check the up-to-date dates on the government website.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Applying to Migrationsverket for a permit as an EU/EEA/Swiss citizen.** You do not need one: you have uppehållsrätt.
- **Mixing up the right to stay with registration.** Staying over a year also means [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).
- **Trusting older guides on permanent permits.** The rule changed on 12 July 2026.
- **Working from the old salary floor.** The median on the day you apply counts: today SEK 34,470 a month.
- **Paying the fee before checking the requirements.** The SEK 2,200 is not refunded.
- **Assuming the family reunification rules are unchanged.** They were tightened in 2026: check before you leave.
