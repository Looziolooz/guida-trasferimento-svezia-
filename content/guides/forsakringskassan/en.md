:::tip[In short]
**Försäkringskassan** (the Swedish Social Insurance Agency) runs child allowance, parental benefit, sickness benefit and housing allowance. What counts is **where you live or work**, not your citizenship. Within the EU the rule is: **you are insured in the country where you work**. **From 1 January 2027 the qualification rules change** for some residence-based benefits: read the warning below.
:::

## Who is insured

Swedish social insurance rests on two grounds: **residence** (you live in Sweden) and **work** (you work in Sweden). Your citizenship does not matter.

Within the EU there is a simple main rule: **you are insured in the country where you work.** If you work in Sweden you are normally insured here, even if your family lives in another country, and the other way round: it depends on your situation.

When you apply for a benefit, **Försäkringskassan checks whether you are insured**. If you do not have a personnummer, it can request a [samordningsnummer](/en/guide/samordningsnummer) for you. For registration, see [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).

:::warn[From 1 January 2027: a new qualification rule]
The Riksdag approved a reform on **20 May 2026**: for certain residence-based benefits you will need to **have lived in Sweden for 5 of the last 15 years**, or to **live in Sweden with earned income** at a certain level for a certain period. Exceptions can be made for "exceptional reasons".

**Examples of benefits affected:** **parental benefit at the basic and minimum levels**, **barnbidrag (child allowance)**, **sickness compensation as guarantee compensation**, **bostadsbidrag (housing allowance)**.

A requirement of **lawful residence** is also introduced for being treated as resident.

**People who settled in Sweden before 1 January 2027 keep the old rules.**

The exact income thresholds for the work route are not in the sources we verified: check Försäkringskassan before deciding when to move.
:::

## Barnbidrag and flerbarnstillägg

**Barnbidrag** (child allowance) is **1 250 kr per child per month**. It is paid **automatically** to people who live in Sweden with their children, normally **up to age 16** (longer if the child is still in compulsory school). It is divided between the parents. **Both parent and child must be insured in Sweden.**

With more children you also get a monthly **flerbarnstillägg** (large-family supplement):

| Children | Supplement per month |
|---|---|
| 2 children | **150 kr** |
| 3 children | **730 kr** |
| 4 children | **1 740 kr** |

## Föräldrapenning

**Föräldrapenning** (parental benefit) covers **480 days per child**, shared between the parents (**660** for twins).

- **390 days** at the **income-based level**: **250 to 1 259 kr per day**.
- **90 days** at the **minimum level**: **180 kr per day**.

:::cost[The 240-day condition]
For the income-based level you need an income that qualifies for **sjukpenning** (the basis is called SGI) of at least **7 083 kr per month** for at least **240 days** before the expected birth. Otherwise you receive **250 kr per day**.
:::

If you have just arrived and have not worked yet, you may miss the higher level: plan before the birth.

## If you fall ill

It depends on how you work. For employees:

1. **Days 1–14**: your **employer** pays **sjuklön** (sick pay).
2. **Karensavdrag**: a deduction of **20 %** of one average week's sjuklön is taken off.
3. **From day 15**: **Försäkringskassan** pays **sjukpenning** (sickness benefit), once your employer has reported you sick to them.

If you are self-employed or unemployed, other rules apply: see Försäkringskassan's website.

## Bostadsbidrag

**Bostadsbidrag** (housing allowance) is for **families with children and a low income**. There is also a form for **young people under 29**. Amounts depend on your situation and are calculated by Försäkringskassan. This benefit is also among those affected by the 2027 rule. For housing in general, see [bostad](/en/guide/bostad).

## How to apply

Försäkringskassan's website has a page for each benefit. Many matters are handled online with electronic ID ([BankID](/en/guide/bankid)). Before applying, make sure you are insured and have a personnummer (or a samordningsnummer assigned for the case).

:::who[Who to contact]
**Child allowance, parental benefit, sickness, housing allowance** → **Försäkringskassan**.
**Sjuklön (first 14 days)** → your **employer**.
**Personnummer / registration** → **Skatteverket**, see [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).
**Work and unemployment** → see [sfi-och-arbete](/en/guide/sfi-och-arbete).
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming citizenship decides**: residence and work are what count.
- **Counting on barnbidrag without being insured in Sweden.**
- **Not checking the 240-day condition** before planning parental leave.
- **Mixing up sjuklön and sjukpenning**: the employer pays the first 14 days, Försäkringskassan from day 15.
- **Forgetting the karensavdrag** when working out the first period of sickness.
- **Ignoring the 1 January 2027 rule**: if you move after that date, the new qualification applies.
