:::tip[In short]
If you plan to live in Sweden **for at least a year**, you must register in the population register (**folkbokföring**) with **Skatteverket**, the Swedish Tax Agency. You notify the move through the **"Flytta till Sverige"** e-service, then go **in person** to a servicekontor for the identity check. It takes **about 5 weeks**, the decision comes **by letter**, and with your first registration you receive your **personnummer**, the Swedish personal identity number.
:::

**Tax:** your tax table depends on where you are registered on 1 November: see [tax and tax return](/en/guide/skatt-och-deklaration).

## The one-year rule

By law, a person is folkbokförd if they can be presumed to sleep regularly in Sweden **for at least a year**. Skatteverket puts it this way: if you have moved and plan to stay a year or more, you **should in most cases be registered**.

If you stay less than a year, you do not register. Further down you will find what to do in that case.

The right to stay is a separate matter, handled by a different agency: see the guide on [right to stay and permits](/en/guide/uppehallstillstand).

## What you have to prove

Skatteverket needs to know that you are legally allowed to live in Sweden. What you show depends on your passport.

| | EU/EEA/Swiss citizen | Citizen of a non-EU country |
|---|---|---|
| **Proof** | Your right of residence (*uppehållsrätt*) | A valid **Swedish residence permit card** |
| **Examples** | Employment contract or certificate of service; own business; sufficient means for at least a year | A permit from another EU country is **not enough** |
| **Address** | Full address where you sleep | Full address where you sleep |

For EU/EEA citizens, **sufficient means** are shown with bank statements, a pension certificate, payslips or home insurance, covering at least a year. If you are looking for work you can stay 6 months to search, but you usually cannot be registered as a jobseeker.

## How to do it, step by step

1. **Notify online.** Use the **"Flytta till Sverige"** e-service on Skatteverket's website. It is available in Swedish, English, Arabic and Ukrainian.
2. **Book the identity check.** You have to go **in person** to a **servicekontor** (Skatteverket's service counter), **booking** at one of the listed offices.
3. **Bring original documents.** All your identity documents, in the original.
4. **Wait.** It takes **about 5 weeks** before the case is assigned to a case officer.
5. **Read the letter.** The decision arrives by letter.

**Family members who move with you** also have to attend the servicekontor in person.

:::cost[Timing]
**About 5 weeks** before the case is assigned to a case officer. The decision comes **by letter**. Registration is **free of charge**.
:::

## Documents for your family

To register a spouse and children, Skatteverket asks for civil-status documents:

- **spouse or partner:** marriage certificate, or an extract from the civil register, or a divorce decree;
- **children:** birth certificate or an extract from the civil register.

Check with Skatteverket whether your certificates must be legalised or translated: it depends on the country that issued them and we cannot tell you in general.

## Your personnummer

The **personnummer** arrives **with your first registration**: you do not apply for it separately. It is the number that identifies you in dealings with Swedish authorities.

:::tip[What it unlocks]
- **Skatteverket ID card.** After registration you can apply for it, but it is for people who are folkbokförd, not for those who only have a samordningsnummer. It is not a travel document. The government has proposed **closing the service**, with a law taking effect on **1 March 2027**: for now that is a proposal, not law.
- **Healthcare at local rates.** To get the reduced rates you must be folkbokförd: a permit alone is not enough. See [healthcare](/en/guide/vard).
- **Bank account and BankID.** Many banks ask for a personnummer: see [BankID and bank account](/en/guide/bankid).
- **Social insurance benefits.** They depend on where you live or work: see [Försäkringskassan](/en/guide/forsakringskassan).
- **AIRE, if you are an Italian citizen.** Registering in Sweden also triggers the duty to register in AIRE (Italy's register of citizens abroad) within 90 days: see [AIRE](/en/guide/aire).
:::

:::note[SFI does not require a personnummer]
The Swedish course for immigrants (**SFI**) does not require a personnummer, and the municipality cannot demand one. See [SFI and work](/en/guide/sfi-och-arbete).
:::

:::warn[Some benefits change in 2027]
If you settled in Sweden **before 1 January 2027**, the old rules apply to certain residence-based benefits. From 2027 new requirements are introduced, one of which is legal residence. The details are in the guide on [Försäkringskassan](/en/guide/forsakringskassan).
:::

## If you stay less than a year

If you stay in Sweden **for less than a year** you do not register. If you need an identification number and have a connection to Sweden, you can apply for a **samordningsnummer**: it is not a personnummer, and it is replaced by the personnummer if you later register. If you stay a year or more, you must notify the move instead. The full details are in the guide on [samordningsnummer](/en/guide/samordningsnummer).

## Common mistakes

- **Not registering despite staying a year or more.** In most cases the law expects you to.
- **Turning up at the servicekontor without a booking.** The identity check is by appointment.
- **Bringing photocopies.** You need your identity documents in the original.
- **Using a permit from another EU country.** For registration only the Swedish card counts.
- **Expecting an answer within days.** It takes about 5 weeks and the reply is a letter: check your letterbox.
- **Forgetting family members.** They also attend in person.
- **Mixing up samordningsnummer and personnummer.** The first is identification only, and does not give residence-based benefits.
