:::tip[In short]
Healthcare is run by the **regions**, which largely set the patient fees themselves: **fees differ from region to region**. Call or go to **1177** for medical advice, and call **112** in an emergency. To get **subsidised care** you need to be **folkbokförd** (registered in the population register): a residence permit alone is not enough. There is **high-cost protection** (**högkostnadsskydd**) for both visits and medicines.
:::

## How it is organised

Public healthcare in Sweden is run by the **regions**. They set most of the patient fees (**patientavgifter**) and organise care locally. So the figures and local rules here are **examples**: always check your own region.

Two numbers and one website will help you all the way.

- **1177**: a phone number for medical advice, open **24 hours a day**. The website **1177.se** lets you find information, book appointments and choose a health centre.
- **112**: the emergency number, for life-threatening situations.

:::warn[Which one do you call?]
**Danger to life or a serious emergency** → **112**. **Not sure how serious it is, or where to go** → **1177**, which gives advice and directs you. Do not go to the emergency department for things that are not urgent: you wait longer and usually pay more.
:::

## The vårdcentral: your first contact

A **vårdcentral** (primary health centre, similar to a family clinic) is where you go for most everyday problems. You normally **register** with one (in Swedish *listning*, meaning you choose which is yours). You can do this through 1177.se or on a paper form. The exact rules, for example how often you can switch, are set by the region.

The hospital's **akutmottagning** (emergency department) is for urgent cases. In between there are **närakut** clinics, urgent care close to home for things that cannot wait but are not life-threatening.

## Who pays a fee and who pays everything

- **If you are folkbokförd**, you are entitled to subsidised care and pay your region's patient fee. What counts is registration, not the permit.
- **EU/EEA/Swiss citizens with a European health insurance card (EU-kort / EHIC)** pay the normal patient fee for urgent and necessary care. **Without a card or certificate you pay in full.**
- **Non-EU citizens who are not registered** pay the **full cost**, except for some countries with agreements.

If you are not registered yet, the route is [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing). If you stay only briefly and cannot register, see also [samordningsnummer](/en/guide/samordningsnummer): note that on its own it does not entitle you to subsidised care.

:::danger[The full cost can be very high]
A hospital stay or operation at full price costs far more than a trip. If you arrive without being registered and without a European card, take out health insurance before you travel.
:::

## Example: Region Stockholm fees

:::cost[Only an example, Region Stockholm]
Other regions have other amounts. The figures below are for **Region Stockholm**.
:::

| Care | Fee (Stockholm) |
|---|---|
| Outpatient visit (vårdcentral and specialists) | **275 kr** |
| Hospital emergency department | **400 kr** |
| Närakut | **375 kr** |
| Appointment cancelled less than 24 hours before | **400 kr** |
| Children and young people **aged 0–17**, and people **aged 85 and over** | **free** |

Source: Region Stockholm and 1177 Stockholm. In other regions both the amounts and the free-care ages may differ.

## The visit cap: högkostnadsskydd and frikort

The **högkostnadsskydd** (high-cost protection) puts a ceiling on what you spend on visits: **at most 1 450 kr in 12 months**. Once you reach it you get a **frikort** (a free-care card that exempts you from fees for the rest of the period). A region may set the ceiling **lower**. Ask your region how you get the frikort.

## Medicines

**Prescription medicines** also have a ceiling. For **2026** it is **3 800 kr in 12 months**, in steps: you pay the full price up to 2 000 kr, and the discounts grow after that. Once you reach the ceiling, you pay nothing more for covered medicines for the rest of the period.

This is separate from the visit cap: two different counters.

## Dental care

Dental care has its own rules, with state support administered by **Försäkringskassan** (see the guide on [Försäkringskassan](/en/guide/forsakringskassan)).

- **Annual grant** (*allmänt tandvårdsbidrag*): **600 kr a year** at ages 20–23, **300 kr** at 24–65, and **600 kr** from age 67.
- **High-cost protection**: you pay the first **3 000 kr** in a year yourself; after that the state covers **50 %** up to 15 000 kr and **85 %** above that.
- **Children and young people**: dental care is free up to an age that **depends on the region**.

Ask for an estimate before expensive treatment.

:::who[Who to contact]
**Advice and guidance** → **1177** (phone or 1177.se).
**Emergency** → **112**.
**Everyday health problems** → your **vårdcentral**.
**Fees and ceilings** → your **region's** website.
**Dental care and grants** → **Försäkringskassan**.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming a residence permit is enough** for subsidised care: registration is what counts.
- **Forgetting the European card (EHIC)** if you are from the EU/EEA and not yet registered.
- **Going to the emergency department for non-urgent things**, instead of calling 1177.
- **Cancelling too late**: an appointment cancelled less than 24 hours before costs money (400 kr in Stockholm).
- **Assuming Stockholm's fees apply everywhere.**
- **Not asking for the frikort** once you have reached the cap.
