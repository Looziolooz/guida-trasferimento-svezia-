:::tip[In short]
A licence from an **EU/EEA** country stays valid in Sweden, even after you register as a resident (*folkbokförd*). A licence from **outside the EEA** can only be used until **one year after you are registered** (the UK and the Faroe Islands are an exception), and it can be **exchanged** only if it was issued by the **UK, the Faroe Islands, Switzerland or Japan**. Everyone else has to take a Swedish licence. If you bring a car you need an **origin check**, possibly customs, an **inspection**, **vehicle tax** and **traffic insurance**. For the exact deadline to register a foreign car, ask Transportstyrelsen.
:::

## Your licence: valid or not?

The authorities are Transportstyrelsen (rules and applications) and Trafikverket (photo, signature and tests). The first question is **where your licence was issued** and **whether you are registered** in Sweden. Registration (*folkbokföring*) is what starts the one-year clock for licences from outside the EEA: see the guide [Registering in Sweden](/en/guide/folkbokforing).

### EU/EEA licence

Transportstyrelsen is clear: *"Körkortet gäller även om du är folkbokförd i Sverige."* (The licence is valid even if you are registered in Sweden.) You can drive on it.

You **can also exchange** it for a Swedish one, if it is valid and you live in Sweden permanently. You apply to Transportstyrelsen with the form *Ansökan om utbyte av utländskt körkort*, then have your photo taken and sign at a Trafikverket office. Whether the exchange is compulsory, by when and what it costs: check Transportstyrelsen's page, as we have not been able to confirm it.

### Licence from outside the EEA

Two rules apply:

1. **Using it:** you may drive on it only until **one year after you were registered** in Sweden. The UK and the Faroe Islands are an exception to this limit.
2. **Exchanging it:** only licences issued by the **UK, the Faroe Islands, Switzerland or Japan** can be exchanged.

For all other countries the rule is stated bluntly: *"Körkort från andra länder utanför EES kan inte bytas ut mot ett svenskt körkort."* (Licences from other countries outside the EEA cannot be exchanged for a Swedish licence.) In plain terms: you have to take a **Swedish licence**. The steps and costs are on the pages of Transportstyrelsen and Trafikverket; check them before you start.

:::warn[Count from registration, not from arrival]
The year is counted from when you were **registered** (*folkbokförd*), not from the day you crossed the border. Write the date down. After it, driving on a licence that cannot be exchanged is no longer allowed, and learning to drive takes time: start well before the year runs out.
:::

## Your licence → what to do

| Where your licence comes from | Valid in Sweden? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **EU/EEA country** | Yes, also after you register | Keep driving. You can exchange it (Transportstyrelsen, then Trafikverket) |
| **United Kingdom** | Yes (exception to the one-year limit) | Can be exchanged for a Swedish licence |
| **Faroe Islands** | Yes (exception to the one-year limit) | Can be exchanged for a Swedish licence |
| **Switzerland** | Up to one year after registration | Exchange it before the year ends |
| **Japan** | Up to one year after registration | Exchange it before the year ends |
| **Any other country** | Up to one year after registration | Cannot be exchanged: take a Swedish licence in time |

## Bringing a car to Sweden

Importing a car is a chain of steps, and each one must be done before the next:

1. **Origin check** (*ursprungskontroll*): compulsory, and valid for **5 years**.
2. **Customs clearance** if the car comes from **outside the EU**.
3. **Registration inspection** (*registreringsbesiktning*).
4. **Vehicle tax** (*fordonsskatt*): it arrives once the car is in use.
5. **Traffic insurance** (*trafikförsäkring*): see below.

:::who[How long can I wait?]
The deadline for registering in Sweden a foreign car belonging to someone who lives here: **ask Transportstyrelsen** before you drive across the border. We have not been able to verify it, and we will not guess.
:::

## Traffic insurance is compulsory

**Trafikförsäkring** is compulsory for a car in use **from the first day**, unless the vehicle is deregistered (*avställd*). It is the only insurance that Swedish law requires. Half and full cover (*halv-/helförsäkring*) are optional extras. Insurance for your home is a different matter: see [Home insurance](/en/guide/hemforsakring).

## Winter tyres

For cars, the rule works like this:

- **1 December to 31 March**: winter tyres are required **when there is winter road condition** (*vinterväglag*): snow, ice, slush or frost on the road.
- **Studded tyres** are allowed **1 October to 15 April**. Outside that period only if winter conditions apply.
- **Do not mix** studded and non-studded tyres.

:::warn[A date is not the whole rule]
Winter tyres are tied to the **road condition** within the period, not just to the calendar: if there is snow, ice, slush or frost on the road between 1 December and 31 March, the rule applies. Do not mix studded and non-studded tyres on the same car. We do not give a minimum tread depth, because the sources disagree: check Transportstyrelsen.
:::

## Congestion tax

**Stockholm and Göteborg** have a **congestion tax** (*trängselskatt*), and it also applies to **foreign cars**. It works automatically: cameras read the number plate and the invoice goes to the **owner** of the car. We do not list amounts, as they depend on place and time: see Transportstyrelsen's page.

## Alcohol and lights

:::danger[The limit is 0.2 per mille]
Driving with **0.2 per mille or more** in your blood is forbidden. It is considered **serious from 1.0**. The limit is very low: the safe rule is not to drive after drinking any alcohol.
:::

**Lights:** while driving, the prescribed lights must be on. In daylight use **dipped headlights or daytime running lights**.

## Moving away again

If you leave Sweden, see [Leaving Sweden](/en/guide/flytta-fran-sverige) for what to settle before you go. For the car, ask Transportstyrelsen.

## Common mistakes

- **Counting the year from arrival.** For licences from outside the EEA it is counted from registration.
- **Assuming any licence can be exchanged.** Only the UK, the Faroe Islands, Switzerland and Japan can; all others must take a Swedish licence.
- **Waiting too long to start learning.** Take the time needed for a Swedish licence into account.
- **Driving a foreign car without checking the deadline.** Ask Transportstyrelsen.
- **Skipping the origin check or the inspection** when importing a car.
- **Forgetting the traffic insurance.** It is compulsory from the first day the car is in use.
- **Thinking winter tyres depend only on the date.** What counts is *vinterväglag* between 1 December and 31 March.
- **Mixing studded and non-studded tyres.**
- **Ignoring the congestion tax** in Stockholm and Göteborg: the invoice goes to the owner.
- **Driving after "just one drink".** The limit is 0.2 per mille.
