:::tip[In short]
A **samordningsnummer** (coordination number) is an identity number for people who **are not and have never been registered** in Sweden. **It is not a personnummer.** You can apply yourself if you will stay **less than a year** and need the number, and an authority can also request one for you. You must go through an **in-person identity check**, by appointment. **It is valid for 5 years.** If you will stay a year or more, the right route is different: [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).
:::

## What it is and what it is not

In Sweden almost everything runs on a ten-digit number: employer, tax, insurance, subscriptions. People who live here long term have a **personnummer** (personal identity number), which you get when you are registered in the population register (**folkbokföring**).

The **samordningsnummer** is for people who **are not and have never been folkbokförd** (registered). It gives authorities and companies a way to identify you, but **it is identification only**. It does not register you as a resident and does not open the rights that depend on residence. If you register later, the samordningsnummer is **replaced by a personnummer**.

## Who can get one

There are two routes.

- **You apply yourself**, if you will stay in Sweden **less than a year**, need the number, and have a connection to Sweden (for example a job, a contract, or a home purchase to register).
- **An authority requests it for you**, for example Skatteverket (the Tax Agency) for work, or Försäkringskassan (the Social Insurance Agency). This is set out in the Act on coordination numbers (2022:1697).

**If you will stay a year or more**, this is not your route: you must report your move and be registered. See [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing). This applies whether or not you are an EU citizen: what counts is how long you stay.

## How to apply

1. **Submit the application.** Either in Skatteverket's e-service "Samordningsnummer" or on paper with **form SKV 7540**.
2. **Book the identity check.** You must appear **in person** at a **servicekontor** (a Skatteverket service office). Book a time in advance. You can hand in the paper form there.
3. **Bring original identity documents.** A passport or valid ID card.
4. **Biometric check.** Skatteverket also performs a biometric check, done at the office.
5. **Wait for the decision.**

Since 2023 Skatteverket carries out the identity checks itself. Coordination numbers come at different levels of assurance, and the highest level requires an in-person check. That is why it cannot all be done from home.

:::tip[Book early]
The step that holds things up is often getting an appointment at the service office. Apply and book **as soon as you know** you need the number, not the day before you start work.
:::

## How long it takes

For a personal application Skatteverket gives **up to 5 weeks** before the case is assigned to a caseworker. Plan for weeks, not days. If an employer or company is waiting for the number, say so right away: in some cases an authority can request it on your behalf.

## Validity, renewal and vilande

- The number is **valid for 5 years.**
- To **renew** it you need a **new visit** and a **new identity check**.
- If you **have not used it** within 5 years, it becomes **vilande**, meaning **dormant**. It is not lost: it can be **reactivated**.

## What it is useful for, and its limits

A samordningsnummer is useful for, for example:

- **working**, so that pay and tax are handled correctly;
- **paying tax** to Skatteverket;
- **insurance** and **subscriptions**;
- **registering the purchase of a home** (lagfart, at Lantmäteriet).

Its limits compared with a personnummer:

- **You are not folkbokförd**, so you do not get the rights that come with registration. See [vård](/en/guide/vard) (healthcare) and [Försäkringskassan](/en/guide/forsakringskassan) for what depends on residence.
- **It does not entitle you to social insurance benefits tied to residence.**
- **Skatteverket's ID card** is for people who are registered, not for those who only have a samordningsnummer.
- **Banks and BankID:** many banks ask for a personnummer, and with a samordningsnummer it can be harder to get an account or electronic ID. Ask your bank before relying on it. See [BankID](/en/guide/bankid).

:::warn[Not a way around registration]
If you stay **a year or more**, you are expected to be registered. Applying for a samordningsnummer to avoid that is not a shortcut: you end up without the rights that registration gives.
:::

:::cost[Fee]
Skatteverket **lists no fee** for a samordningsnummer application, unlike the ID card, which costs 400 kr. Timing: up to **5 weeks** before your case is assigned to a case officer.
:::

## When you register later

When you move here for good and are registered, you get a **personnummer** and the samordningsnummer is replaced. From then on you use only the new number. You do not need to close anything yourself, but tell your bank, employer and insurers that your number has changed.

:::who[Who to contact]
**Application, identity check, biometrics** → **Skatteverket**, via the e-service or a servicekontor (by appointment).
**If an authority needs it** (work, benefits) → ask that office whether it requests the number for you.
**If you will stay a year or more** → see [folkbokföring](/en/guide/folkbokforing).
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Applying when you will stay a year or more.** Then you need folkbokföring.
- **Forgetting to book the in-person check.** The online application alone is not enough.
- **Expecting a resident's rights.** Subsidised healthcare and Försäkringskassan benefits follow registration, not the number.
- **Counting on BankID or an account straight away.** It can be harder with a samordningsnummer: check with the bank.
- **Leaving it unused for 5 years** and then being surprised that it is **vilande**. It can be reactivated, but it takes a procedure.
- **Not telling anyone when the number changes** to a personnummer.
