:::tip[In short]
For electricity in Sweden you need **two contracts**: the **supplier** (*elhandel*) is your choice, the **grid** (*elnät*) is not. If you don't pick a supplier you are given a **default contract** (*anvisat avtal*), which usually costs more: **choose a supplier when you move in**. For broadband there is no law limiting how long you can be tied in, so read the contract. Many mobile operators ask for a personnummer for a subscription: ask first. No TV licence bill arrives: the fee is part of your taxes. And bottles and cans with a deposit go back to the shop.
:::

## Electricity: two contracts, not one

The Swedish Energy Markets Inspectorate (Ei) says you need *"ett elhandelsavtal och ett elnätsavtal"*, that is, a supply contract and a grid contract.

| | **Supplier (*elhandel*)** | **Grid (*elnät*)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | You buy the electricity itself | The network that brings it to your home |
| **Do you choose?** | **Yes**, you can compare and switch | **No**, it is the network for your area |

You usually get two separate bills. When you move, the one you have to decide on is the supplier.

:::warn[If you don't choose, you often pay more]
If you don't choose a supplier you get an **anvisat avtal**. According to Ei the price is *"ofta högre"*, often higher. **Choose a supplier when you move in**, not after the first bill.
:::

## Contract types

Ei describes three main types. Hourly-price contracts have largely been replaced by quarter-hour ones.

| Type | How the price works |
|---|---|
| **Kvartspris** (quarter-hour price) | A price for each quarter of an hour, set in advance |
| **Månadspris** (monthly price) | The price changes every month and is set afterwards |
| **Fast pris** (fixed price) | Fixed price for **1, 2 or 3 years** |

No type is best for everyone: it depends on how predictable you want your bill to be. A fixed price is the easiest to plan around; the others follow the market.

### How to compare

Use **Elpriskollen** (elpriskollen.se), the comparison site run by the Energy Markets Inspectorate. It is **independent**: it doesn't sell contracts, it compares them.

:::tip[What to do]
1. Find out whether the home already has an electricity contract or whether you have to sign one yourself (check your rental contract or ask the landlord, housing association or manager; see [Housing](/en/guide/bostad)).
2. Compare suppliers on Elpriskollen.
3. Pick a contract type and sign **before** you move in, or as soon as you arrive.
:::

## Broadband: tie-in periods and delivery

For broadband **there is no law setting how long you can be bound** by a contract. The length is whatever the contract says: **read it before you sign** and check the tie-in period and what happens if you want to cancel.

For fibre installation contracts, Konsumentverket (the Swedish Consumer Agency) says that **delivery should take place within 24 months** of the contract. If your contract says something different, or delivery doesn't come, you can get help from **Telekområdgivarna**, the telecom advice service.

:::note[If you rent]
In many buildings the landlord or housing association arranges the connection. Ask before you sign anything of your own.
:::

## Mobile phone

For mobile phones the terms depend on the operator. One thing to know: **many operators ask for a personnummer for a subscription**. If you don't have one yet, **ask the operator** what alternatives exist before you choose. For how to get the number, see [Population registration](/en/guide/folkbokforing).

Other services have similar requirements, such as a bank account and BankID: see [Bank account and BankID](/en/guide/bankid).

## There is no TV licence bill

In Sweden **no TV licence bill** is sent to your home. There is instead the ***public service-avgift***, which you **pay through your taxes**:

- it is paid by anyone **over 18** who lives and pays tax in Sweden, **even without a TV**;
- it is **1 % of your income, at most 1 184 kr in 2026**;
- the maximum is reached at an income above **118 400 kr**.

So you don't have to register anywhere. For how taxes and the tax return work, see [Tax and tax return](/en/guide/skatt-och-deklaration).

## Public transport

Each region has its own ticket system and its own fares. As an example, in Stockholm **SL** runs the metro, buses and commuter trains:

- you can pay by holding a **contactless card** (Visa, Mastercard or American Express) to the reader;
- or you can use an **SL-kort** card.

We give no prices here because they change: you'll find them on the transport company's site for your region. If you live outside Stockholm, look up the regional operator and check which payment methods it accepts.

## Pant and recycling

**Pant** is a deposit you get back when you return empty packaging. Cans and bottles with the **Swedish pant label** go back to collection points, often in **supermarkets**, and you get the deposit refunded. In 2025 the deposit on PET and aluminium went up. Throw them in the bin and you throw away money.

Otherwise you sort **paper, plastic, metal, coloured glass and clear glass**. For how it works in your building, ask the landlord or housing association.

## Common mistakes

- **Not choosing an electricity supplier.** You end up on the default contract, which often costs more.
- **Mixing up supplier and grid.** You choose the supplier; you can't change the grid.
- **Signing a broadband contract without reading the tie-in period.** No law limits it.
- **Assuming you can get a mobile subscription without a personnummer.** Ask the operator first.
- **Looking for a TV licence bill.** It never comes: the fee is in your taxes.
- **Throwing away cans and bottles with the pant label.** Take them to a collection point.
- **Trusting transport prices from a forum.** Always check the regional operator's own site.
