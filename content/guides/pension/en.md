:::tip[In short]
The Swedish pension has **three parts**: the public pension (*allmän pension*, made of *inkomstpension* and *premiepension*), the **guarantee pension** (*garantipension*) for people with little or no pension, and the workplace pension (*tjänstepension*). Every year you get the **orange envelope** showing what you have earned so far. If you have pension in **two EU countries**, you apply in **the country where you live**: it forwards the application to the other, and **each country pays its own part**. Careful: the **guarantee pension is paid only to people who live in Sweden**.
:::

## The parts of the Swedish pension

A pension in Sweden is not a single payment. It is made of several parts that add up.

| Part | What it is | Can you take it abroad? |
|---|---|---|
| **Inkomstpension** (public) | Part of the public pension (*allmän pension*) | Yes, in any country |
| **Premiepension** (public) | Part of the public pension (*allmän pension*) | Yes, in any country |
| **Tilläggspension** | Supplementary public pension | Yes, in any country |
| **Garantipension** | For people with a low or no public pension; based on **residence** in Sweden | **No: only if you live in Sweden** |
| **Tjänstepension** | The pension from work, through your employer | Ask your employer or pension fund |

:::note[Workplace pension]
The pension from work usually comes **through your employer**, often when there is a collective agreement (see [Unions and unemployment insurance](/en/guide/fack-och-a-kassa)). We do not go into individual plans here: check what your employer or pension provider has sent you.
:::

## The orange envelope and minpension.se

If you have earned public pension during the year, the Swedish Pensions Agency (Pensionsmyndigheten) sends you an **annual statement**: the **orange envelope** (*orange kuvert*). It arrives **from mid-January to mid-March** and you can also find it on **Mina sidor**.

Read it: it shows what you have earned so far. To see **everything in one place**, use **minpension.se**, which gathers your Swedish pensions (public, workplace and private). But remember that **pension you earned in another country usually does not show up** there. You have to ask the authority in that country.

## At what age can you retire?

There is no single age at which the pension "kicks in". For the public pension you choose when to start, within limits.

| What | Age |
|---|---|
| **Riktålder** (guiding age) | **67**, from 2026 to 2032 |
| Earliest for the public pension (inkomst- and premiepension) | **64** in 2026, that is 3 years before the riktålder |
| **Garantipension** | From the riktålder, so **67** |

The age depends on the part: the public pension can be taken three years before the riktålder, the guarantee pension only from the riktålder.

## What changed in 2026

For 2026, **inkomstpension** and **tilläggspension** went up by **1.9 %**. The **garantipension** changed slightly, by between **−94 and −3 kr a month** depending on the level (Pensionsmyndigheten, *Så blir pensionen 2026*). Always look at the figure in your own orange envelope.

## Pension from two EU countries

If you have worked in Sweden and in another EU country, you are entitled to **a pension from each country**: the periods are counted together, but **each country pays only its own part**.

### How to apply

1. **Apply in the country where you live.** That country forwards the application to the other. You do not need to file two separate applications.
2. **Apply in good time.** The Swedish agency says **up to a year ahead**; minpension.se says **six months ahead** is usually enough.
3. **Expect 4–6 months** for a decision.

### What to have ready

- the **countries** where you worked and the **periods**, **from age 15**;
- your **foreign social security numbers** for each country where you worked.

### Example: Italy and INPS

Italy is only an example: the same pattern applies to other EU countries. Italy's INPS uses **totalizzazione**: periods of contributions in other EU countries **count towards qualifying for a pension**, with at least **one year** of contributions. Then each institution pays **pro quota**, that is, in proportion to the periods in its own country.

For other countries, ask the country's pension authority how Swedish periods are treated. If you plan to **retire to Italy** after living in Sweden, read our sister site's guide [Italia Pratica, pensioners](https://italia-pratica.it/en/guide/pensionati).

## Living abroad with a Swedish pension

:::warn[The guarantee pension is lost if you move]
The **garantipension** is based on **residence in Sweden** and **is paid only to people who live in Sweden**. Since **October 2022 it is no longer granted to anyone living in another EU/EEA country**. The only exception is for people **born in 1937 or earlier**. If you have a low public pension and count on the guarantee pension, **moving abroad can make you lose it**. Talk to the Pensions Agency **before** you move.
:::

What you **keep in any country**:

- **inkomstpension**;
- **premiepension**;
- **tilläggspension**.

If you live abroad you must send a **levnadsintyg** every year, a certificate that you are alive.

### Tax on a pension paid abroad

Swedish pensions paid to people living abroad are normally taxed under **SINK**, the special income tax for non-residents: **22.5 % in 2026** and **20 % from 1 January 2027**, with an **allowance of 3,799 kr a month** in 2026. How to apply is on the Skatteverket website: see also [Tax and tax return](/en/guide/skatt-och-deklaration). The full picture for people leaving Sweden is in [Leaving Sweden](/en/guide/flytta-fran-sverige).

:::note[Double taxation]
Which country taxes the pension depends on the type of income and on the **tax treaty** between the two countries. Have a tax adviser look at it before you move.
:::

## What to do, in order

1. **Open the orange envelope** every year (or check Mina sidor).
2. **Check minpension.se** to see all your Swedish pensions.
3. **Write down** your work periods in each country, with the foreign insurance numbers.
4. **Apply** 6–12 months ahead, in the country where you live.
5. **If you may move away**, first check what happens to the guarantee pension and to SINK.

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking the Swedish pension is a single payment.** It is several parts with different rules.
- **Not opening the orange envelope.** It is your yearly check: errors and gaps show up there.
- **Believing minpension.se shows foreign pensions.** Usually it does not.
- **Filing two separate applications** when you have pension in two EU countries: you apply in the country where you live.
- **Waiting until the last month.** A decision takes 4–6 months.
- **Assuming the guarantee pension follows you abroad.** It is paid only to people who live in Sweden.
- **Forgetting the yearly levnadsintyg** if you live outside Sweden.
- **Forgetting tax.** A pension paid abroad is normally taxed under SINK; also look at the treaty between the countries.
