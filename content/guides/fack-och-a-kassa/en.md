:::tip[In short]
Sweden has **no statutory minimum wage**: minimum pay is set in **collective agreements** (*kollektivavtal*) between unions and employers. A **union** (*fack*) and an **a-kassa** are **two separate memberships**: you can have one without the other. The a-kassa is unemployment insurance, and under the rules in force since 1 October 2025 **your benefit level (80, 60 or 50 %) depends on how long you have been a member**. If you work in Sweden, **join an a-kassa as early as you can**: once you have lost your job it is too late to reach a higher level.
:::

## How the Swedish labour market works

Sweden has no law that sets a minimum wage. Minimum pay and many other terms of work are set in **collective agreements** signed by trade unions and employer organisations.

How common are they? According to the Mediation Office (Medlingsinstitutet), **88 %** of employees aged 18 to 68 were covered by a collective agreement in 2025 (**83 %** in the private sector), and **just under 70 %** were union members. Even if you are not a member, your employer's collective agreement can still apply to your job: ask whether there is one.

## Union and a-kassa: two different things

This is the most common mistake among newcomers: thinking "the union" and "the a-kassa" are the same. They are not. Sveriges a-kassor says so plainly:

> *"Du behöver inte gå med i facket för att gå med i a-kassan, och tvärtom."*
> (You do not have to join a union to join an a-kassa, and vice versa.)

| | **Union (*fack*)** | **A-kassa** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Organisation of employees in a sector or profession | Unemployment insurance fund |
| **What it gives** | Collective agreement and help in workplace disputes; often extra benefits | Benefit if you lose your job and meet the conditions |
| **Who can join** | Usually people working in the union's sector or profession | Sector and profession funds have their own criteria; **Alfa-kassan** is open to anyone who has worked in Sweden, in any occupation |
| **Cost** | Depends on the union and often on your income | Depends on the fund; example: **Alfa-kassan 170 kr per month** |
| **Tax-deductible?** | **No**: "Du kan inte få något avdrag för din fackföreningsavgift" (Skatteverket) | Not stated here: ask your fund |

:::note[Check the fees yourself]
We do not list union fees: they depend on the union and often on your income. Look at the website of the union for your sector. The Alfa-kassan figure (170 kr per month) comes from its own website and may change.
:::

## The union: finding the right one

Swedish unions are organised by sector or profession and belong to three large confederations:

- **LO**: blue-collar workers;
- **TCO**: white-collar employees;
- **Saco**: university graduates.

To find yours, look for the union for your **sector or profession** on the confederation's website, or ask colleagues or the **skyddsombud** (safety representative) at your workplace.

### What a union does

- negotiates the **collective agreement** for your sector;
- **helps you in disputes** with your employer.

Many unions also offer **extra income insurance** (*inkomstförsäkring*). Terms vary: **ask** what it covers, what it costs and from when it applies.

## A-kassa: the rules in force since 1 October 2025

The **a-kassa** (*arbetslöshetskassa*) pays a benefit to people who lose their job. Since **1 October 2025** the system is **income-based**.

### The conditions

The **reference period** (*ramtid*) is the **12 months before the month you apply**. In that period you must have had:

- at least **120,000 kr of income**;
- at least **11,000 kr per month in at least 4 months**.

Monthly income counts up to a maximum of **34,000 kr**, and you must be **at least 20 years old**.

### How many days you get

| In the reference period... | Days |
|---|---|
| at least 11,000 kr a month for **11–12 months** | **300** |
| at least 11,000 kr a month for **8–10 months** | **200** |
| at least 11,000 kr a month for **4–7 months** | **100** |
| you do not reach 120,000 kr in total, but earned at least 11,000 kr in **4 consecutive months** | **66** |

### The benefit level depends on your membership

| Membership of the a-kassa | Level |
|---|---|
| Member for **at least 12 months in a row**, covering the whole reference period | **80 %** |
| Member for **at least the last 6 months** | **60 %** |
| All other cases, **even if you are not a member** | **50 %** |

:::warn[Why you should join early: every month counts]
For **80 %** you must have been a member for **all 12 months** before you apply. For **60 %**, the last 6 months are enough. **Joining only after you have lost your job does not make up for lost time**: you start at 50 %. Example: if you start working in Sweden in January, join in July and lose your job in December, you do not yet have 12 months of membership. If you join in your **first month of work**, each passing month brings you closer to the full level. A monthly fee costs little compared with the gap between 80 % and 50 %.
:::

**If you are not in any fund**, you can still receive the 50 % level: non-members apply through **Alfa-kassan**.

## How to claim

1. **Register with Arbetsförmedlingen on your first day of unemployment.** The rule is blunt: *"Du kan inte få ersättning från a-kassan för dagar som du inte varit inskriven hos Arbetsförmedlingen"* (you cannot receive a-kassa benefit for days when you were not registered with Arbetsförmedlingen).
2. **Apply to the a-kassa** through **Mina sidor**.

:::danger[Do not wait]
The day you register with Arbetsförmedlingen matters: days **before** registration are not paid. If you lose your job, registering is the first step, even before you have gathered all your papers.
:::

For Swedish courses, registering as a jobseeker and job search, see the guide [SFI, work and a-kassa](/en/guide/sfi-och-arbete).

## Have you worked in another EU/EEA country?

Periods of work and insurance in another EU/EEA country can be **added** to your Swedish periods using the **U1** certificate (*Intyg U1 – för sammanläggning av perioder*). It is issued by the **authority of the country where you worked**. The easiest route: **ask your a-kassa**, which requests it electronically.

## Workplace basics

The rules on types of employment are in the Employment Protection Act (LAS):

- **Tillsvidareanställning**: a permanent contract.
- **Provanställning**: a probationary period of **at most 6 months**.
- **Särskild visstidsanställning**: a fixed-term contract that becomes **permanent after more than 12 months in total within 5 years**.

**Holiday:** by law you have **25 days' holiday a year** (the Annual Leave Act, *semesterlagen*). If you start work **after 31 August**, your first year gives you **5 days**.

**Skyddsombud:** the employees' safety representative, usually appointed by the union (Work Environment Act, ch. 6).

:::who[Who to contact]
**Contract, pay, dispute** → the union for your sector.
**Unemployment benefit** → your a-kassa (or Alfa-kassan).
**Registering as a jobseeker** → Arbetsförmedlingen.
**Questions about a-kassa rules** → the fund and IAF's pages.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Thinking union and a-kassa are the same thing.** They are two separate memberships, each with its own fee.
- **Waiting to join an a-kassa.** Without 12 months of membership you will not reach 80 %.
- **Believing membership is enough.** You must also meet the income conditions and apply on Mina sidor.
- **Registering with Arbetsförmedlingen late.** Days before registration are not paid.
- **Forgetting the U1 certificate** if you have worked in another EU/EEA country.
- **Expecting to deduct union fees from tax.** That is not possible.
- **Signing without knowing whether there is a collective agreement.** Ask the employer or the union.
- **Forgetting the limits on temporary contracts.** Probation is at most 6 months; *särskild visstid* becomes permanent after more than 12 months in 5 years.
