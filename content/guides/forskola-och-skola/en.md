:::tip[In short]
The municipality must offer a place in **förskola** (preschool) from **age 1**, **within 4 months** of your application. From **age 3** your child gets at least **525 free hours a year**. Compulsory school starts **in the autumn of the year the child turns 6** (*förskoleklass*) and continues with **grades 1–9**. Education is **free, school lunch included**. **Gymnasium** (upper secondary school) is free. And if your family speaks another language at home, your child may be entitled to **mother-tongue tuition**.
:::

This guide is for families moving to Sweden from any country. The rules are in the Education Act (*skollagen*, 2010:800). Where the law is clear we quote it; where practice depends on the municipality we tell you whom to ask.

## Age and school at a glance

| Age / moment | What | In short |
|---|---|---|
| From **age 1** | **Förskola** (preschool) | Place offered by the municipality within 4 months; fee with a cap (*maxtaxa*) |
| From **age 3** | **Allmän förskola** | At least 525 free hours a year |
| From the autumn of the year the child turns **6** | **Förskoleklass** | First compulsory year |
| After förskoleklass | **Grundskola**, grades **1–9** | Free, with free school lunch |
| Before and after school hours | **Fritidshem** | After-school care; fee set by the municipality |
| After grundskola | **Gymnasieskola** | Free; you can start up to the first half of the year you turn 20 |

## Förskola: preschool

### From when, and how fast

The law says: *«Barn ska från och med ett års ålder erbjudas förskola i den omfattning det behövs.»* (Children must be offered preschool from age one, to the extent needed.) "Needed" means, for example, that the parents work or study, or that the child needs preschool for their own development.

The **municipality** must offer the place **within 4 months** of your application. So apply **early**, not when you need a place next week. You apply to the municipality where you live: search for "förskola" or "barnomsorg" on its website.

### If you are unemployed or on parental leave

Children of **unemployed** parents or parents on **parental leave** are also entitled to preschool: at least **3 hours a day or 15 hours a week**. It is not full time, but the child is not left without a place.

### From age 3: 525 free hours

From **age 3** there is **allmän förskola** (general preschool): at least **525 free hours a year** for every child.

### What it costs: the maxtaxa

The preschool fee is called **maxtaxa**. It is a **percentage of the household income**, with a **national cap**. It is lower for the second and third child, and free from the fourth. Your municipality works out the exact amount: **ask them for the fee**. We give no figures because they change.

## Compulsory school

### Where it starts

Compulsory school starts **in the autumn of the year the child turns 6**, with **förskoleklass**. Then comes **grundskola**, **grades 1–9**. Compulsory school applies to all children **living in Sweden**, whatever their citizenship.

:::note[The structure changes from 2028]
A reform that has already been passed will in future replace förskoleklass with a 10-year compulsory school, with effect **from 2028**. For now, förskoleklass at age 6 still applies.
:::

### Free, lunch included

The law is plain: *«Utbildningen ska vara avgiftsfri.»* (Education must be free of charge.) And on food: *«Eleverna ska utan kostnad erbjudas näringsriktiga skolmåltider.»* (Pupils must be offered nutritious school meals at no cost.)

So you pay **no fee** for teaching and none for school lunch. If a school asks you to pay for attendance, ask which rule it relies on.

### Fritidshem

**Fritidshem** is after-school care for schoolchildren. Unlike school, the fee is set by the **municipality**: ask the school or the municipality.

### Mobile phones at school

In 2026 a law on collecting pupils' mobile phones at school was **proposed**. We cannot tell you how it will work: **ask your child's school** how it handles phones.

## Mother tongue

:::tip[Another language at home? Your child may be entitled to mother-tongue tuition]
A pupil is entitled to **mother-tongue tuition** (*modersmålsundervisning*) if the language is **used every day at home** (*dagliga umgängesspråk*) and the child has **basic knowledge** of it. This applies to any language: **Italian**, for example, qualifies, as do Arabic, Polish or Spanish. For the **national minority languages** the right always exists.

The municipality organises the lessons if there are enough pupils to form a group. **You apply to the school or the municipality.** Do it at the start of the school year, not when the term is already under way.
:::

If you are unsure whether your language qualifies or whether there are enough pupils, **ask anyway**: the school and the municipality give the answer.

## Newly arrived pupils

Children who arrive in Sweden without knowing Swedish get **language support**. What it looks like (reception classes, Swedish as a second language, help in the subjects) depends on the municipality and the school. **Ask the school** what support exists, ideally at the very first meeting.

## Gymnasium

**Gymnasium** is the upper secondary school after grundskola. The law says: *«Utbildningen i gymnasieskolan ska vara avgiftsfri.»* (Education in gymnasium must be free of charge.)

You can start **up to the first half of the year you turn 20**. For young people who arrive as teenagers that margin matters: ask the municipality which programmes are open to someone newly arrived.

## First steps, in order

1. **Register** if you are staying at least a year: see [Population registration and personnummer](/en/guide/folkbokforing).
2. **Apply for preschool** as soon as you can: the municipality has 4 months.
3. **Sort out Swedish for yourself**: courses for adults are in [SFI, work and unemployment insurance](/en/guide/sfi-och-arbete).
4. **Read up on parental leave, allowances and child health care** in [Pregnancy, children and family](/en/guide/barn-och-familj).
5. At the school, ask about **mother-tongue tuition** and **support for newly arrived pupils**.

:::who[Whom to ask]
**Preschool place, fritidshem, mother tongue, gymnasium** → your municipality (*kommun*).
**Questions about the school day, lunch, support** → your child's school.
**General rules** → Skolverket and the Education Act.
:::

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting to apply for preschool.** The municipality has up to 4 months to offer a place.
- **Assuming the unemployed have no right.** With unemployed or parental-leave parents there are at least 15 hours a week.
- **Confusing preschool with school.** Preschool is an entitlement; compulsory school starts in the autumn of the year the child turns 6.
- **Paying for teaching or school lunch.** Education and meals are free by law.
- **Forgetting to ask for mother-tongue tuition.** The right exists if the language is spoken every day at home, but you have to ask for it.
- **Waiting until you are 20 for gymnasium.** You can start up to the first half of the year you turn 20, not later.
- **Treating the phone proposal as law.** In 2026 it was only proposed: ask the school.
